COMMENTS TO THE AUTHOR(S)
I congratulate the authors for responding to the previously raised issues as well as independently detecting an additional problem and correcting that also. This is now a stronger manuscript, however I do have a couple of points that I think would be worth addressing. The first is the method used to correct the earlier shortfall regarding whether the greater proportion of people in proximity to strict PAs is less than those in proximity to non-strict PAs is due to the number of PAs. The authors have looked at the areas of each category and then the number of people in proximity but I think a more effective way would be to look at the PA boundary distance instead of area. Secondly, I see a clear systematic bias with the comparison of the WorldPop vs GHSL estimates with ALL WorldPop estimates for people inside PAs being higher than their GHSL equivalents. Yet this is not mentioned. I think this needs to at least be mentioned and addressed in the discussion – even if the underlying reasons are unclear. I have detailed these more below.
Pg 5 Ln 7-8: It would be worth putting the % of population for those inside PAs (18.8 million) in 2020, as has been done for the 257.6 million inside or within 10km (15.4%).
Pg 5 Ln 16-22: I appreciate that the authors have addressed this issue as it makes a substantial difference to understanding the situation. However, I think the more appropriate way to measure this would have been comparing the total boundary lengths of strict vs non-strict PAs instead of the total area as, for example, many small non-strict reserves might be the same total size (in area) as a single large strict reserve, but the total boundary length would be much greater, affording increased possibility for people to live within 10km buffer (almost a reverse edge-effect!). If this is something that could be accomplished without too much difficulty – which I suspect it is given that all sites must have boundary lengths as attributes -  it might be worth adding to the analysis.
Pg 6 Ln 12: Again, put the % here as it helps the reader to quickly understand the magnitude of the missing data.
Figure 3: I’m not sure how this shows “broad agreement” at the Inside PA level as ALL of the estimates for this are above the line (they do not “cluster around the 1:1 line”), indicating that the WorldPop estimate is systematically higher than the GHSL estimate. This is reflected in Pg 11 Ln 8-9 but needs to be clearly stated within the figure as well. And since it is a systematic issue, it should be discussed (ideally on Pg 13 where these datasets are compared).
