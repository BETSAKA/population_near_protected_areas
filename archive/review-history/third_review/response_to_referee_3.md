### General comment on strict versus non-strict protected areas

Reviewer comment:

> The authors have looked at the areas of each category and then the number of people in proximity but I think a more effective way would be to look at the PA boundary distance instead of area.

Response:

We agree that total boundary length is a relevant complementary diagnostic for proximity-based exposure. We therefore extended the analysis to compute total PA boundary length by IUCN category from the same filtered WDPA geometries used in the manuscript. The new results confirm the previous interpretation: non-strict PAs remain more populated than strict PAs after standardization by total boundary length as well as by area.

In the revised manuscript, we now report both diagnostics. For the 2020 all-PA cross-section, non-strict PAs are associated with about 999 people inside or within 10 km per km of PA boundary, compared with about 891 for strict PAs. We also added a short methods note in the Supplementary Materials explaining how this boundary-length diagnostic was computed.

### Figure 3

Reviewer comment:

> I’m not sure how this shows “broad agreement” at the Inside PA level as ALL of the estimates for this are above the line ... indicating that the WorldPop estimate is systematically higher than the GHSL estimate. This ... needs to be clearly stated within the figure as well. And since it is a systematic issue, it should be discussed.

Response:

We agree and revised both the figure treatment and the surrounding text.

The previous wording was too broad. In the revised version, the Results section now distinguishes clearly between the two panels: agreement is described as relatively close for the broader “inside or within 10 km” perimeter, whereas the “inside PAs” panel is explicitly described as showing a systematic upward shift of WorldPop relative to GHSL.

We also modified Figure 3 itself by adding panel-specific annotations that summarize the bias pattern numerically. In the revised figure, the inside-PA panel reports that 64 of 75 country estimates lie above the 1:1 line, with a median WorldPop-to-GHSL ratio of 1.87. By contrast, the broader perimeter is much closer to parity, with a median ratio of 1.01.

Finally, the Discussion now addresses this systematic asymmetry explicitly. We note that we cannot determine the precise cause from our data alone, but that the pattern is consistent with differences in how the two gridded products spatially allocate very small populations within PA interiors.
